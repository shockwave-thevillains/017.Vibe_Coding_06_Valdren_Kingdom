# Valdren

An explorable medieval kingdom that runs in the browser. It is built with Three.js
as a 100 % static site (HTML, ES modules and JSON), with no build step, backend or
API keys, so it can be hosted on GitHub Pages as is. Every model and texture is
generated in code at load time; the project ships no art assets.

> **Status: complete (all five phases), then expanded twice.** The engine core,
> the whole kingdom, its people with branching dialogue, ten secrets that add up
> to one mystery, a journal, compass and maps, saved progress, a running
> day-night cycle and Low / Medium / High graphics presets. The first expansion
> doubled the town (houses, a bar and a town hall you can walk into, townsfolk
> and children, animals, hot-air balloons you can ride). The second widened the
> kingdom to 1.3 km across, with a lake and a harbour town full of ships, a
> lighthouse, a riverside quarter, the village of Millbrook, the princess and her
> garden, a dragon over the mountains, many more birds and street cats, and a
> prettier sky, water and light. Since then: a hand lantern, your own cottage
> with a bed to sleep in, music composed as you play, and the Milky Way across
> the night sky. The kingdom has no religious buildings or symbols, so it sits
> comfortably with every faith.

## The kingdom

- **Castle Valdren** crowns the hill to the north: an octagonal curtain wall with
  eight round towers, a twin-towered gatehouse with a raised portcullis, a cobbled
  courtyard with barracks, stables, a well and training dummies, the great
  donjon (with a sealed, chained door), and the **Throne Hall**, which you can
  walk into: columns, a long carpet, a two-step dais and throne, stained-glass
  windows, banners, braziers and a chandelier.
- **The walled town** below: an oval wall with sixteen towers and four gates,
  winding cobbled streets with kerbs, lamp posts and bunting, about eighty
  half-timbered and stone houses with gardens, woodpiles, washing lines and
  chicken coops behind them, and:
  - the old **market square** with stalls, a well and a king's statue;
  - ***The Broken Oar***, a bar you can walk into: a counter with bottles and
    casks, tables and benches, a hearth and a chandelier;
  - the **Town Hall** with its clock tower, open to visitors (a council table,
    shelves of ledgers, the arms of Valdren);
  - **Fountain Square** in the east quarter, with a two-tier fountain, benches,
    stalls and the **bakery** (its domed oven glowing);
  - the **stables** and a paddock in the west quarter, the blacksmith's
    **forge**, and the old **burial ground**: plain grassy earth mounds, each
    marked only with a ring of white stones;
  - about twenty homes you can step into, each with a hearth, a bed, a table
    and stores. Walk through any open doorway.
- **Outside the walls:** the royal road winding up the castle hill, a
  three-arched stone bridge over the river, the **Riverside** quarter along the
  north bank and a suburb on the road east, wheat, barley and cabbage fields with
  fences, a farmstead with a barn, haystacks and a pig sty, a turning windmill,
  woods to the west, north-east and south-west, a ruined watchtower on a knoll at
  the forest edge with a hermit's camp beside it, and the **Balloon Meadow**
  beyond the east gate.
- **The Royal Garden** beside the castle: clipped hedges, a rose arch, flower
  beds, a lily pond, a pavilion, benches and a swing, where the princess walks.
- **Mirrormere and Aldra's Haven:** the river runs east into a wide lake. On its
  west shore stands the harbour town of Aldra's Haven, with its own houses,
  three wooden piers, a crane, fish stalls and crates on the quay; cogs, a
  carrack and fishing boats sail the lake, barges and rowboats work the river,
  and boats lie moored at the piers and pulled up on the banks. A **lighthouse**
  watches the north shore.
- **Millbrook**, a village down the south road: thatched and timbered cottages
  round a green with a great oak, a well and a duck pond, and ***The
  Millstone***, an inn you can walk into.
- **Dragon's Peak** towers over the north-western mountains, with the lair of
  **Old Ember**, the dragon, on a ledge high on its flank.

## The people

Seven people are at the heart of the story. Each has a place, a routine and a
story to tell, and what one of them lets slip can open up another.

| Who | Where | Doing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **King Osric II** | On the dais of the Throne Hall | Pacing before the throne, hands behind his back |
| **Dame Isolde Varn**, Captain of the Guard | Castle courtyard | Walking her rounds in helmet and breastplate |
| **Brannoc**, Blacksmith | The forge, north-west of the market | Hammering at the anvil |
| **Maera**, keeper of *The Broken Oar* | Behind the bar counter | Polishing a tankard |
| **Tobias Fenn**, merchant of Saltmere | The east stalls of the market | Strolling between his stalls |
| **Wynn Hollis**, farmer | The farmstead across the river | Walking out to the ploughed field, hoe in hand |
| **The Old Hermit** | His camp beside the ruined watchtower | Pottering between the fire and the ruin |

Around them some thirty-five townsfolk go about their day, and all of them will
chat: guards at the gates, three regulars at the Broken Oar's tables, Hilde
kneading dough in the bakery, the Town Hall's clerk, a fruit seller and a toy
seller, a town crier, a stablehand and a carter at the paddock, walkers on the
streets, Captain Gale the aeronaut on the Balloon Meadow, and seven children:
four playing tag round the fountain, one racing down the main street, one
watching the horses and one fishing off the end of a pier.

Further afield you will meet **Princess Elinor**, the King's daughter, strolling
round her garden with her lady-in-waiting Lady Rosamund and her guard Brenna
(she has things to say about her uncle, and about the dragon); Corwin the
harbour master, Nessa the fisherwoman and Jonas the sailor in Aldra's Haven;
Morwen, keeper of the lighthouse; Gwen behind the Millstone's bar and Old Tom on
the village bench; and Hob the shepherd with his flock.

Walk up to someone and a prompt appears (their name also floats above their
head). Press `E`, or tap **Interact**, to talk. The camera moves over your
shoulder, the line types itself out (click, tap or press `Space` to show it at
once), and you pick a reply with the mouse, a tap or the keys `1`–`3`. `Esc`
ends the conversation.

## Animals, ships, balloons, the dragon and your lantern

- **Animals** live all over the kingdom: horses in the paddock (Chestnut likes a
  scratch; Ash bites), sheep on the meadows, cows by the windmill, pigs in their
  sty, chickens at the farm and by the coops in town, ducks on the river and the
  Millbrook pond, pigeons pecking about the market, Fountain Square and the quay,
  stray cats on the streets (and one asleep by the Broken Oar's fire), and dogs
  (Biscuit at the market, Patch chasing the children, Rusty at the farm).
  Overhead fly flocks of birds: gulls over the lake and the harbour, crows over
  the town and the castle, swallows above Millbrook, ravens round the dragon's
  peak. Chickens, sheep, cats, pigeons and ducks shy away if you rush at them.
  Walk up to a dog, cat, horse, sheep, cow or pig and press `E` to pet it.
- **Ships and boats** sail Mirrormere and the river, rolling and pitching on the
  water, while others lie at their moorings.
- **Old Ember**, the dragon, circles the mountains and the valley, gliding and
  beating her wings, banking into her turns, now and then breathing a plume of
  fire at the clouds, and every so often landing at her lair to rest before she
  takes off again.
- **Your lantern:** press `F` (or tap the lantern button) to light a brass
  bullseye lantern and again to put it out. You hold it out in front of you; its
  lens throws a beam like a torch where you face, tilting with the camera, and
  when you stand still you turn to shine it wherever you look. After dark the
  beam shows faintly in the air. It works by day and indoors too, and the first
  nightfall of a visit reminds you it is there.
- **Hot-air balloons** drift high over the valley. On the Balloon Meadow, beyond
  the east gate, a tethered balloon waits on the grass: stand by its basket and
  press `E` (**Ride**) to go up on its rope for a look over the whole kingdom.
  It comes back down by itself after about a minute.

## Your cottage, sleep and music

- **Your cottage** stands just inside the south gate, a short walk from where the
  journey begins, with a lamp post by its door. It is marked with a little green
  house on the map and the minimap.
- **Sleeping:** stand by the bed in your cottage and press `E` (**Sleep in your
  bed**). After dusk you sleep until seven the next morning; by day you rest until
  seven in the evening. The screen fades out and back, your lantern goes out, and
  the game is saved.
- **Music** plays from the moment you press Begin: a gentle dance on recorder and
  lute by day, a slow harp air over a soft drone at night, and a lively jig with
  a frame drum inside the Broken Oar and the Millstone. Nothing is recorded: the
  instruments are synthesised and the tunes are made up as they play (from a
  mode, a chord progression and a motif that returns), so they never quite
  repeat. After two or three phrases a tune ends and the music rests for a while.
  It goes quieter while the game is paused and falls silent while you sleep. Set
  the volume (or turn it off) with the **Music** slider on the title screen or in
  the pause menu; the choice is remembered.
- **The night sky:** after dark the Milky Way arches across the southern sky, a
  band of faint light with a warmer core, ragged edges and dark dust lanes, the
  stars thicker within it. Clouds drift across it, and it fades at dawn.

## The mystery

Valdren is hiding something about the Red Winter, thirty-one years ago, when the
King's elder brother died in a fire. Ten things around the kingdom tell the real
story: two monuments, two banners, four written pages, a grave and a sealed
door. Each one still to be found catches the light with a faint golden glint.
Stand by it and press `E` (or tap **Interact**) to read or examine it; it goes
into your journal.

- **Journal** (`J`, or the **Journal** button): your discoveries in the order you
  found them, everything people have told you under *Rumours*, and, once all ten
  are found, *The Truth*. What you have read also changes what some people will
  say to you, and the story has an ending to reach in conversation.
- **Compass, clock and minimap:** the compass and the time of day sit at the top;
  the round minimap turns with the camera and marks people (gold once you have
  met them) and your discoveries. Click or tap it, or press `M`, for the full
  map of the kingdom.
- **Day and night:** the sun crosses the sky in about fourteen minutes and the
  night passes in about five. Windows and street lanterns light up after dusk.
- **Saving:** progress (discoveries, conversations, where you stand, the time of
  day) is saved in this browser as you play, and the title screen offers to
  continue or to start a new journey. If the browser blocks storage the game
  still runs; it just cannot remember you.

## Graphics quality

The title screen and the pause menu (`Esc`, or the pause button on a phone)
offer three presets. Touch devices start on **Medium**, everything else on
**High**; the choice is remembered in this browser.

| | Low | Medium | High |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shadows | **off** | 1024² map, 34 m around you | 2048² map, 46 m around you |
| Resolution (device pixel ratio cap) | 1 | 1.5 | 2 |
| Edge smoothing (MSAA) | off | on | on (4×, in the passes) |
| Lantern lights at night, hearth lights indoors | none | 2 | 4 |
| People drawn within | 40 m | 60 m | 90 m |
| Grass tufts around you | 2,500 | 6,000 | 18,000 |
| Full-detail trees within | 40 m | 55 m | 75 m |
| Textures | 256 px | 256 px | 512 px |
| Bloom (glowing lanterns, windows, sun) and vignette | off | off | on |
| Shadows from your lantern's beam | off | off | on |

On High the picture is drawn through a short chain of passes (bloom, then tone
mapping and a light vignette), and its edges are smoothed in those passes
rather than by the canvas. Shadows, resolution, lights, people, grass, tree
distance and bloom change at once.
Texture size, tree and crop detail and edge smoothing are fixed when the world
is built, so the menu says when those will follow on the next load. If the game
runs below 24 frames per second for ten seconds, it suggests a lower preset
(once per visit).

## Run it locally

ES modules do not load from `file://`, so serve the folder over HTTP:

```bash
python -m http.server 8000
```

Then open <http://localhost:8000/>. On a phone on the same network, open
`http://<your-computer-ip>:8000/`. The page needs internet access, because
Three.js loads from jsDelivr.

It runs in current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari (16.4 or later, for import
maps), on desktop and on phones, with WebGL 2.

### Developer options

- `?debug` shows a performance readout (FPS, draw calls, triangles, position).
  `F3` or the backtick key `` ` `` toggles it in game.
- `?time=HH` starts at a given hour (0–24), for example `?time=18` for sunset or
  `?time=22` for night (instead of the saved time).
- `?quality=low`, `medium` or `high` starts with that preset, overriding (but not
  changing) the saved choice. The choice itself is stored under the
  `valdren.settings.v1` key, apart from the journey.
- `window.valdren` exposes the renderer, scene, world, player, people, lore and
  the save in the browser console.
- To forget your progress, use *Start a new journey* on the title screen, or
  remove the `valdren.save.v1` key from the site's local storage.

## Technical notes

- **Three.js r170** (npm `three@0.170.0`). The core and the addons are loaded from
  jsDelivr through an `<script type="importmap">` pinned to that exact version.
- All paths are relative, so the site works from `username.github.io/<repo>/`.
- **Terrain:** a single 384 × 384 grid mesh, 1,280 m across, sampled from a
  height function: rolling meadows, the castle hill, the town shelf, farmland,
  the ruins' knoll, terraces for the harbour, the village and the garden, a ring
  of craggy mountains, the dragon's peak, a carved river and the lake basin.
  Roads cut and fill the land along smoothed profiles. Gameplay height queries
  interpolate the actual mesh triangles, so feet always meet the rendered
  ground. Steep and high ground is painted as layered rock, with snow on the
  ledges near the summits.
- **Procedural textures:** stone, cobbles, plaster, timber, planks, roof tiles,
  thatch, dirt, soil, bark, ground detail and water ripples are painted into data
  textures at load time, each with a matching normal map. The banner arms and the
  tavern sign are drawn with the 2D canvas API.
- **Sky:** a shader dome with the sun and moon, stars at night and drifting
  clouds (layered value noise projected onto a flat layer overhead, lit from the
  sun's side, coloured by the hour: white at noon, gold and pink at sunset, faint
  and moonlit at night). The Milky Way is a Gaussian band about a fixed great
  circle, its width and brightness broken up by noise along the band, brighter
  and warmer towards its core, with a dust lane of darker noise down its middle;
  the star threshold drops inside it so the stars crowd there.
- **Music** (`src/music.js`): Web Audio only. A lookahead scheduler queues one
  bar at a time a third of a second ahead. Instruments are built per note from
  oscillators, filters and envelopes: a plucked string (saw and triangle through
  a closing low-pass), a recorder (sine with a breathy band of noise and delayed
  vibrato), a frame drum (a falling sine thump and a noise tap), a tambourine, and
  a drone (tonic and fifth through a slowly swelling low-pass). Everything goes
  through a reverb made from decaying noise and a gentle compressor. Each theme
  is a mode, a tempo, an eight-bar chord progression, arpeggio and drum patterns
  and a set of melodic rhythms; melodies land on chord tones on the downbeat,
  move by step between them, repeat the piece's motif in bars 1-2 and 5-6, and
  end each phrase on the tonic. The game chooses the theme from the time of day
  and whether you are inside an inn.
- **Sleep:** your cottage is the walk-in house nearest the start; furnishing a
  room records where its bed is, and the bed becomes something to interact with
  inside that room. Sleeping sets the clock, puts you beside the bed and saves.
- **Water:** the river and the lake carry the water depth in each vertex. The
  shallows turn clear and green, a line of foam marks the banks, and two layers
  of ripples drift different ways so their tiling never shows; reflections come
  from the sky's image-based lighting.
- **Post-processing (High):** `EffectComposer` with a multisampled half-float
  scene target, `UnrealBloomPass` (its threshold drops after dusk so lanterns and
  windows glow, while by day only the sun and hard glints bloom) and a last pass
  that tone-maps, converts to sRGB and adds a light vignette.
- **Lighting:** physically based materials, ACES filmic tone mapping, and
  image-based lighting from a slightly desaturated copy of the sky. One
  shadow-casting directional light follows the player, with its shadow frustum
  snapped to shadow-map texels so shadows do not shimmer. The sky's horizon
  colour drives the fog. Windows and lanterns glow after dusk.
- **Batching:** static architecture is merged per material and per 160 m chunk,
  so the whole kingdom is about 90 meshes. UVs are projected in world space, so
  textures keep their real-world scale on every wall.
- **Instancing:** trees, bushes, crops, market stalls, roof-tile courses, fence
  posts and rails, barrels, crates, gravestones and boulders are `InstancedMesh`es.
- **Trees** come in two levels of detail. Both are drawn instanced, and the shader
  keeps each tree in exactly one of them depending on its distance from the
  camera, so detail changes per tree rather than per chunk. Foliage close to the
  camera dissolves instead of filling the screen.
- **Grass** is drawn entirely on the GPU: one instanced tuft mesh wraps around the
  player, reads the terrain height and a density/colour mask from textures, and
  sways in the wind. Wheat, leaves, flags and the river surface move too.
- **The traveller** is one skinned mesh built in code and bound to a 19-bone
  skeleton: a lofted torso, limbs, hands and boots, a pleated tunic skirt and a
  cloak, and a head sculpted from a sphere (brow, eye sockets, nose, cheekbones,
  lips, jaw and chin). The face (eyes, brows, lips, skin shading, stubble) is
  painted onto a canvas at load time. Hair and beards are a shell around the
  head whose edge fades out through a strand mask, so the hairline breaks into
  strands instead of ending in a hard rim. The walk and run cycles are
  procedural: pelvis sway and twist, counter-rotating shoulders, bending knees
  and elbows, heel-to-toe feet and a trailing cloak. The same `createFigure()`
  builds every NPC with its own colours, hair and beard (about 12k triangles
  each).
- **People** are built from `data/npcs.json` with the same `createFigure()` as
  the player: a look (colours, build, robe or tunic, hair, beard, age lines, a
  burn scar, a gloved hand) and an outfit of procedural accessories (crown,
  ermine collar, nasal helm, breastplate, pauldrons, sword, aprons, hats, a
  cowl, a hammer, a tankard, a staff, a hoe). Accessories are baked
  into the figure's own skinned mesh, so each person costs four or five draw
  calls; their shadows are dropped beyond 24 m, and beyond the preset's range
  they are hidden (they keep to their routines unseen). Poses (hands behind the
  back, hammering, polishing, leaning on a staff, sitting, drinking, kneading)
  are layered over the walk cycle; seated poses lower the hips onto the bench.
  People turn their heads to watch you pass and gesture while they speak.
  Children (`"child": true`) are the same figure at about 60 % scale with a
  larger head.
- **Dialogue** trees live in the same file. Each node is a line and two or three
  replies; a reply leads to another node or ends the conversation. Nodes and
  replies can require and set flags (`"if": ["heard_edmund", "!met_king"]`,
  `"set": ["heard_chains"]`), which is how hearing something from one person
  changes what you can ask another. `met_<id>` is set after a first meeting,
  and `"start"` can pick a different greeting for a return visit.
- **Lore** lives in `data/lore.json`: ten entries, each tied to a named spot the
  world builds (`anchor`), with a kind (scroll, monument, banner, grave, door),
  its text, and the traveller's own note; plus the rumour lines that dialogue
  flags unlock, the closing *Truth* and an epilogue. Finding an entry sets the
  flag `lore_<id>`, and all ten set `lore_complete`, which conversations can test.
  The props (the founding stone, a lectern, a ledger page, a song sheet, a
  letter, the burnt standard, heather on a grave) are part of the batched world
  geometry; the glints are a single point cloud.
- **Rooms you can walk into** are houses whose ground floor is a shell: walls
  with a doorway, a plank floor and a beamed ceiling, an open door and a step up
  to the threshold. The floor is a walkable surface and the room an interior,
  so the camera stays under its ceiling; above the ceiling the house is solid
  to the camera. Furniture (tables, benches, stools, chairs, beds, chests,
  shelves of pots, books or bottles, hearths with chimney breasts, rugs, a bar
  counter, a bread oven, chandeliers) is batched with the rest of the town.
  While you are inside, the pool of point lights moves to the room's hearth and
  candles, day or night, and the window panes glow with daylight.
- **Animals** (`src/animals.js`): each species is one `InstancedMesh` built
  from procedural parts. Every vertex carries a part id and a pivot, and the
  vertex shader (also used for the shadow pass) swings the legs in a trot,
  lowers the head to graze or peck, wags tails and flaps wings from four
  per-instance values; lying down folds the legs. On the CPU each animal
  wanders inside its pen or patch, grazes, rests, shies away, or trots after
  someone, checking the same colliders and steps as the player.
- **Boats** (`src/boats.js`): hulls lofted from a few cross-sections, with
  planked decks, masts, yards, rigging and striped square sails, one mesh per
  vessel: rowboats, fishing boats, a river barge, cogs and a carrack. Each one
  follows a loop on the lake, a stretch of river back and forth, or stays at a
  berth, and rolls and pitches with the water.
- **The dragon** (`src/dragon.js`): one mesh (body, neck, tail, head with jaw and
  horns, legs and membrane wings) bent and flapped entirely in the vertex shader
  (and its shadow pass) from a few uniforms: wingbeat, folding, the sway of
  neck and tail, the jaw. On the CPU she picks waypoints over the realm, banks
  and pitches into her turns, glides or beats her wings by how she is climbing,
  breathes a cone of fire every half minute or so, and every seventh flight
  lands at her lair.
- **The lantern** (`src/lantern.js`): a small merged mesh (body, cone and
  chimney, handle, lens tube) placed at the right hand every frame, a glowing
  lens and horn panes, a `SpotLight` (warm, 42 m reach, soft edge, a slight
  flicker) placed at the lens and aimed along the traveller's heading, pitched
  with the camera and eased so it lags a little, and an additive open cone for
  the visible shaft after dark. The spotlight is always in the scene and only
  its intensity changes, so lighting the lantern never recompiles a material;
  on High it casts shadows, and its shadow map is only redrawn while lit. A
  `lantern` pose (layered like the staff) holds the arm out.
- **Balloons** (`src/balloons.js`): one mesh each (a lathed envelope striped per
  face, skirt, ropes, wicker basket, sandbags, burner) and a flame that roars
  in bursts. Four follow wide loops above the land; the tethered one climbs to
  about 34 m on its rope with the player standing in the basket, sways a
  little, and lands again.
- **Day and night:** the clock runs continuously. The sky, fog, sun, moon and
  ambient light follow it every frame, while the image-based lighting is only
  rebuilt when the sky has visibly changed (often at dawn and dusk, never at
  noon or midnight). A small pool of point lights (four on High, two on Medium,
  none on Low) moves to the lanterns nearest the player after dark, so the light
  pools on walls and cobbles cost the same anywhere in the kingdom.
- **Maps** are painted once at load from the world itself: the terrain's own
  colours with hill shading, the river, forests drawn as tree marks, the fields,
  and every wall and building taken from the collision shapes, on a parchment
  tint. The minimap draws a rotated window of that picture; the full map adds
  place names, the people you have met and your discoveries.
- **Saving** uses `localStorage` (key `valdren.save.v1`), and every access is
  wrapped in `try`/`catch`. It is written after each discovery and conversation,
  every 20 seconds of play, on pause and when the page is hidden. A saved
  position is only restored if it is still standable ground.
- **Collision:** circles and rotated boxes with a vertical extent, in a uniform
  spatial grid. Walkable surfaces (the bridge deck, the hall floor and dais, room
  floors and door steps) sit above the terrain; steps up to 45 cm can be climbed; deep water and slopes above
  45° cannot. The camera pulls in rather than pass through walls, and stays under
  the ceiling of whatever room the player is in.
- **Budget:** one shadow-casting light, the device pixel ratio capped at 2, and
  the presets above. Switching presets re-seeds the grass, resizes or drops the
  shadow map and adds or removes lantern lights in place; the lit materials
  recompile once for the new light setup. Each animal species, balloon and
  boat costs one or two draw calls. In town at street level, High draws about
  340 to 400 calls and 1.8 to 2.1 M triangles a frame (shadow pass and bloom
  included), Medium about 310 to 375 calls and 1.6 to 1.9 M, and Low (no shadow
  pass) about 235 to 270 calls and 1.1 to 1.3 M, depending on how many people,
  houses and boats are in view; at the harbour it is 0.3 to 0.7 M. (Measured
  with a software renderer, which counts the work but cannot time it.)
- **Work that cannot be seen is skipped,** without changing what you see:
  - the terrain is drawn as 16 tiles and the river as 6 stretches, all sharing
    one set of vertex buffers, so the parts outside the view are culled;
  - each frame the animal instances hold only the animals in view, or close
    enough to cast a shadow into it, so a flock on a far meadow costs nothing
    until you look at it;
  - the sky is drawn last, on the far plane, so its clouds and stars are only
    worked out where no building, hill or tree covers them;
  - walkable surfaces (floors, piers, the bridge) are kept in a grid, so a
    height query, which every person, animal and step check makes, looks at a
    handful of them rather than all;
  - hidden people and static scenery skip their per-frame matrix updates.
  Together this cut about 12 % of the triangles in town, a third to a half at
  the harbour, and two thirds of the animals' CPU time.
- **The same world on every preset:** tree shapes and crop density depend on
  the preset, so where things stand is drawn from separate random streams; the
  forest, the rubble and every collider are identical on Low, Medium and High.

## Deploy to GitHub Pages

1. Push this repository to GitHub.
2. Open **Settings → Pages**.
3. Under **Build and deployment**, choose **Deploy from a branch**, then pick the
   branch that holds these files and the `/ (root)` folder, and click **Save**.
4. After a minute or two the game is live at `https://<username>.github.io/<repo>/`
   (for this repository: `https://shockwave-thevillains.github.io/017.Vibe_Coding_06/`).
   The **Actions** tab shows the "pages build and deployment" run while it
   publishes.

Nothing needs building: GitHub serves the files as they are (`.nojekyll` turns
Jekyll off), every path in the project is relative, and Three.js comes from
jsDelivr. A free account can publish Pages from a public repository; a private
repository needs a paid plan.

To check the sub-folder setup before publishing, serve the parent directory and
open the game from its folder, the way GitHub Pages will:

```bash
cd ..
python -m http.server 8000
# then open http://localhost:8000/<repo-folder>/
```

## Troubleshooting

- **"Still loading…" or "Could not download the game scripts":** the page could
  not reach `cdn.jsdelivr.net`. Check the connection, or a blocker that filters
  CDNs.
- **"WebGL is not available":** turn on hardware acceleration in the browser
  settings, or try another browser.
- **Slow or hot phone:** choose **Low** in the pause menu, then reload for the
  lighter textures as well.
- **Progress is not kept:** private windows and some privacy settings block
  `localStorage`. The title screen says so; the game still runs.
- **404 on GitHub Pages:** wait for the "pages build and deployment" run to
  finish, check that Pages points at the right branch and `/ (root)`, and open
  the address with the repository name exactly as GitHub shows it.
